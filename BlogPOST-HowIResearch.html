How I Kill My Own Findings

Four years of gold research produced five results worth keeping. Two of them make money. Three are statistically solid and worthless. This is the machinery that told them apart.

Most published trading research shows you what survived. The interesting part is what killed everything else, because that is the only transferable skill. I keep a ledger of every experiment on this project — roughly a hundred and thirty pre-registered trade structures across gold, crypto, indices and energy — and the ledger's most valuable pages are the ones recording how a promising number turned out to be nothing.

Here is the scoreboard, ranked by strength of evidence rather than by profitability, because that distinction is the whole point.

Finding                                      Evidence type    Tradable?
Swap-free gold instrument                    contractual      YES
Diversification + binding-constraint rule    measured         YES
"Zigzag" bottom detector, 80% precision      strong           no
Regime accuracy edge, +2.12pp vs baseline    strong           no
Intrabar-path information, above a p99 null  strong           no

Three of five are real, carefully measured, and produce no money. If you take one thing from this piece, take that ratio.

The controls, and what each one killed

These accumulated one at a time, each after something silently manufactured a result that evaporated when the control was added. They are run before any new backtest is believed.

1. Floor every cost at the live broker quote

Historical spread columns understate reality, and not by a little. Measured against live quotes:

Asset       CSV models    live      error
NATGAS         4.0 bp    199.4 bp     50x
HEATOIL        4.0 bp     80.1 bp     20x
PALLADIUM      5.0 bp     56.4 bp     11x
BRENT/WTI      ~3.0 bp    8-9 bp      ~3x
gold H4        $0.30      $0.448      1.5x

A first pass of one study crowned a three-asset book the best in the sweep on a 20x cost subsidy. At honest cost the third sleeve collapsed from +0.47 to +0.09 Sharpe and the entire agricultural complex went negative. Costs in basis points, never dollars: ten cents of slippage is 0.002bp on bitcoin and 12bp on Brent, and on EUR/USD a dollar-based cost model once produced a Sharpe of −7.7 out of a units error alone.

2. Benchmark against buy-and-hold over the same window

A long-only trend follower in a bull market posts a fine Sharpe with zero timing skill.

Window            buy & hold gold    the trend follower
2008+ (18.4y)              +0.57               +0.64
2022+ (4.4y)               +1.15               +1.11

It never beats owning the metal — its Sharpe just tracks gold's own Sharpe in whichever window you pick. What it actually sells is drawdown control (−10.0% against −23.1% post-2022), not alpha. A later test made this sharper still: a degenerate "always hold maximum volatility-targeted long" policy scores +1.075 against the strategy's +1.090, statistically indistinguishable at matched volatility.

3. Walk-forward the universe, not just the parameters

Choosing which assets to trade using the full sample roughly doubles apparent Sharpe. Same signal, three levels of honesty:

always-in, full-sample asset choice        +1.03
weekend-flat                              +0.61
walk-forward top-8, re-picked each Jan    +0.26

4. Quote a standard error

At 4.4 years, the standard error on a Sharpe ratio is about 0.6. So a post-2022 "+1.07" has a 95% interval of roughly [−0.11, +2.25] — not distinguishable from the full-sample +0.44, nor from zero. Short windows cannot resolve a Sharpe, and most of the disagreements in this field are people quoting point estimates at each other inside overlapping confidence intervals.

5. Trade the label with perfect hindsight before building a detector

This is the control I would give away first, because it costs five minutes and it retroactively explained five failed studies.

Before building a model to detect something, trade that something with perfect foresight and see what it is worth. Five separate studies here tried to detect gold's bottoms. The oracle says:

Perfect knowledge of...              value as an overlay
geometric BOTTOMS                          +0.017
geometric BOTTOMS, standalone long         -0.675
geometric TOPS                             +0.259
forward 12-bar DOWN window                 +4.001

Perfect bottom-detection is worth +0.017 — and −0.675 traded standalone. The reason is embarrassing once you see it: the strategy is long-only and near-always-in, so it is already long at bottoms. All the value was on the other side, in trimming before bad windows. Five studies optimised the worthless half of the problem. No amount of model quality can lift a ceiling.

6. Deflated Sharpe AND probability of backtest overfitting AND walk-forward re-selection

All three, together, never a raw Sharpe. The instructive case: a cross-asset divergence signal whose best full-sample configuration scored +0.547. Re-selecting the configuration each year on past data only collapsed it to +0.238. The catch came not from testing the winner's robustness but from walk-forwarding the selection process itself.

And the two statistics are not redundant. One bracket search returned a deflated Sharpe of 0.0000 while its overfitting probability looked reassuringly low, because that grid's variation was a monotonic cost gradient rather than a genuine edge gradient.

7. Check whether the question is answerable before answering it

The newest control, and the one that saved the most wasted work. Before building a detector, count the independent events, invert the economics to the accuracy you would need, and put the standard error next to it.

required AUC for a worthwhile edge          0.617
best AUC ever achieved here                 0.593
gap                                        +0.024
standard error of AUC at 247 events        0.0367
expected best-of-8 lift from PURE NOISE    +0.053

I had designed an eight-way comparison of feature representations to detect a +0.024 effect, in a design whose pure-noise floor was +0.053. The experiment was strictly incapable of answering its own question. Count decided, non-overlapping events — not bars, and not windows: at a five-day horizon, 46% of windows never touch either barrier and must be dropped, which overstated my sample by 1.9x when I counted windows.

Three controls I had to add this year

Gate de-risking overlays at matched volatility. Anything that only ever reduces exposure will look bad on a naive paired test, because it gives up return along with risk. Levering the candidate back to the baseline's volatility first changed one round's verdict from 0 of 29 passing to 3 of 29.

Compare your best rule to the best rule a no-edge dataset would give you. I searched 21,300 conditional triggers and the winner scored a z-statistic of 11.5, which sounds decisive. Then I block-bootstrapped the null distribution of the maximum across the whole search: its median is 9.34. A z of 9 is unremarkable in a search that size. This also proves that searching harder is a losing move — the bar rises faster than the statistic.

Run the whole pipeline on synthetic noise. This one caught me directly. On pure Gaussian returns, one arm of my representation test beat the baseline by +0.032 bits per event with 7 of 9 years positive — which would have passed my own pre-registered bar. The statistic turned out to be biased toward the higher-capacity arm, and a single synthetic draw cannot establish a null. Without that control I would have published a representation "edge" made of nothing.

Every one of these exists because it caught me. None of them is theoretical.

What is tradable

1. A swap-free gold instrument

One broker carries a gold instrument with swap_mode = DISABLED — literally no overnight financing — that tracks spot gold at 0.998 daily-return correlation.

                     standard gold    swap-free version
overnight financing       -3.70%/yr           0.00%/yr
spread                 $0.42 (0.94bp)     $0.55 (1.23bp)
sleeve net Sharpe           +0.862             +1.065

+0.203 Sharpe for 0.33bp of extra spread. The obvious objection — a perpetual instrument must charge its funding somewhere, presumably in the price — was tested rather than assumed: the log basis against spot drifts +0.17%/yr across 2,711 hourly bars, inside a 1% band. Nothing resembling the 3.70% it avoids.

This ranks first because the gain is contractual. A saving of 3.70% a year is 3.70% a year whether the strategy works or not; it needs no out-of-sample validation. Set against it: every signal-side improvement ever attempted on this book has been worth exactly zero.

Caveats, since they matter: the instrument has six months of history, so re-measure the basis monthly; and only 5 of that venue's 68 symbols are swap-free, none of them an index, crypto or energy — so the win is confined to gold.

2. Diversification, and matching the book to the binding constraint

Book                                  net Sharpe    maxDD
single-sleeve gold (standard)             +0.862    -22.8%
single-sleeve gold (swap-free)            +1.065    -19.7%
four sleeves, equal weight                +1.096    -10.5%
four sleeves, gold at 50%                 +1.212    -11.5%

Sleeve correlations sit between +0.04 and +0.14 — the diversification is real, not a curve-fit. The second half is a rule about risk limits: the best book depends on which rule actually fails you. On a prop account killed by a daily loss limit, widening from four sleeves to nine takes pass rate from 88.6% to 94.1% and daily breaches from 9.3% to zero, by thinning the left tail. Where no daily limit binds, the narrower higher-Sharpe book wins instead.

What lost, and this surprised me: hierarchical risk parity, random-matrix covariance cleaning, minimum-CVaR and inverse-volatility weighting — 0 of 20 configurations beat equal weight. The classic 1/N result. Estimation error swamps the optimisation gain.

What is not tradable — and these are the careful ones

3. The bottom detector: 80% precision, worth nothing

operating point    precision    random base    lift
recall 22%              71%          33.5%      2.1x
recall 10%              75%          33.5%      2.2x
recall  5%              80%          33.5%      2.4x

Precision-recall AUC 0.60. Hand-written rules cap out near 62%, so the model genuinely adds something. Traded as "enter on the flag, exit at the first reasonable profit", all 48 grid cells are negative and the honest walk-forward number is −0.76 against buy-and-hold's +0.79, with zero of eight years better. And per control 5, the target was worthless before the detector existed.

Read the lift, not the headline: widening the tolerance window inflates absolute precision by raising the random base rate. The 2.1–2.4x lift is the honest statistic.

4. The regime accuracy edge: statistically solid, five ways to trade it, none worked

accuracy predicting next-day direction        51.97%
persistence baseline (NOT 50%)                49.85%
edge                                          +2.12pp
block-bootstrap 90% interval          [+0.51, +3.69pp]
probability the edge is <= 0                     1.5%
years positive                              8 out of 9

Beating persistence rather than a coin flip is the part most published work skips. Then: standalone (Sharpe −0.44), as an overlay, as a confidence gate at every threshold, across five forecast horizons, and an 80-cell stop/target grid in which 79 of 80 cells were Sharpe-negative. Also tested and adding nothing over this: multi-scale Hurst exponents, a causal hidden Markov model, wavelet energy decomposition, and a twelve-symbol intermarket panel.

5. Intrabar path: real information that will not convert

The newest result. Slice each four-hour bar into its sixteen fifteen-minute constituents and describe the path price took inside it — realised volatility, how much distance was travelled, how many times direction changed, and crucially when the high and low occurred. Open-high-low-close tells you the extremes but never which came first.

That block carries genuine incremental information: +0.0142 bits per independent event, above its shuffled-alignment null's 99th percentile, 7 of 9 years positive, with precision of 0.600 against a 0.427 base rate. It is the first input in four years to beat a properly-constructed information null.

It does not trade. As a position-trimming overlay, 12 of 16 configurations clear a matched-volatility t-statistic of +1.50, one reaches +2.39, and the best raw Sharpe is +1.379 against the strategy's +1.098. Still dead: every configuration is negative in 2018-21 and positive in 2022-26. That is a regime artifact across sixteen cells, not noise, and the overfitting probability is 0.687 against a 0.30 bar. Applied at book level instead it went 0 of 32.

The activity pattern explains why it is fragile. The signal fires 0.7 to 2.1 times a week, and 51% of firings last a single four-hour bar. Bolting a one-bar flicker onto a multi-week trend follower multiplies turnover from 18.5 trades a year to 106. And no individual feature clears its own standard error — the strongest is 0.530 against an SE of 0.0367 — so the block beat its null while the mechanism remains a plausible story rather than an established one.

The pattern

Two of five made money, and neither came from predicting the market. Both came from measuring what a position costs — a swap table and a correlation matrix. Costs are published, contractual, deterministic, and need no out-of-sample validation. Signals are estimated, noisy, and mostly imaginary.

Three of five are genuine, statistically careful positives that produce no profit: 80% precision, an accuracy edge with a bootstrap interval clear of zero, and incremental information above a p99 null — and between them, roughly a hundred and thirty failed trade structures.

Information is necessary and not sufficient. The conversion step is where the money goes.

One more thing, because it is the most common suggestion I get: combining the components does not help. Five independent refutations here — confluence stacking of twelve chart-pattern concepts, a six-component published stack, feature-block stacking, combining two independent books (which made things worse, 1.41 to 1.26), and the intrabar overlay at book level. Stacking raises precision. On this asset precision has never been the failing variable.

If you are starting out: measure what you pay before optimising what you predict, and build the machinery that kills your findings before you build the findings.
