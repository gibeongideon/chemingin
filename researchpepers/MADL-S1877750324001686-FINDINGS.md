# Findings: "Mean Absolute Directional Loss as a new loss function for machine learning problems in algorithmic investment strategies"

**Paper:** Michańków, Sakowski & Ślepaczuk (2024), *Journal of Computational Science* 81:102375 — `researchpepers/1-s2.0-S1877750324001686-main.pdf`.
**Tested:** 2026-11-06 with `scripts/v5_madl_lstm.py`.
**Verdict:** does not replicate. The exact loss cannot train a network, and every loss ends up "always long".
**Repo record:** `V5_FINDINGS.md` §3dj.

## What the paper claims

- MADL = mean( −sign(R × R̂) × |R| ): minus the return of trading the sign of the forecast.
- An LSTM on daily returns (single feature), walk-forward 4y train → 1y test, long/short on the sign.
- Tuning and training with MADL beats MAE on BTC and crude oil (USO), in risk-adjusted returns out-of-sample.

## How we tested it

- **Model:**
  - LSTM 2 × 64, returns only;
  - sequence 20 (BTC) / 10 (WTI, gold);
  - 4y train with 33% validation and early stopping; 1y test, retrained every year.
- **Assets:** BTC D1 (OOS 2016+), WTI D1 (2002+, the paper's oil), gold D1 (2002+).
- **Losses:**
  - MADL_EXACT (the paper's);
  - MADL_SOFT (tanh, a trainable version — the paper's own suggested extension);
  - MAE and MSE;
  - UNTRAINED (random weights, never trained).
- **Robustness:** 5 seeds each. Costs: BTC 0.05%, WTI 0.03%, gold 0.01% per position change.
- **Benchmark:** buy-and-hold. On an asset that drifts up, a long/short rule must beat "always long" to show any skill.

## Results (out-of-sample net Sharpe, median of 5 seeds)

| | BTC | WTI | Gold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buy & hold | **+0.89** | −0.02 | **+0.70** |
| MADL (paper) | +0.82 | −0.02 | +0.66 |
| MADL soft (trainable) | +0.87 | −0.13 | +0.56 |
| MAE | +0.31 | −0.08 | +0.50 |
| MSE | +0.87 | −0.21 | +0.56 |
| Untrained network | +0.41 | −0.02 | +0.19 |

## What it means

1. **The paper's loss cannot train.** The sign function has zero slope, so gradient descent receives nothing. A "MADL-trained" network is just its random starting weights. Its seeds come out at exactly +buy-and-hold or −buy-and-hold, identical to the untrained control: a random LSTM outputs a nearly constant sign, so it is always long or always short depending on the seed. The paper's MADL-vs-MAE difference is a lucky constant position versus a trained model.
2. **No loss beats simply holding.** Trained models on BTC and gold learn to be long 85–97% of the time and match or trail buy-and-hold. On WTI, which has no drift, everything is about zero. Direction hit rates are 50–53%.
3. **Inside the champion book** (Sharpe 1.26), gating the XAU/BTC sleeves with the forecast does nothing for MADL and MSE (they never say "short" after 2018). The MAE gate hurts (1.10).

**Takeaway:** the loss function is not the lever. The champion already captures the drift that these models rediscover.

## Follow-up: borrowing the idea into ZigZag (§3dk)

**What we tried:** retrain the live ZigZag model on what each trade earns: "did the live trade started here make money?", weighted by how much it made or lost. Before, it learned "is this bar a bottom?".

**Script:** `scripts/v5_zigzag_madl.py`, Switch gold H1, walk-forward 2024-01..2026-11.

| Variant | Sharpe (lock / no lock) | Random-timing median | Beats random? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Live model (bottom label) | 0.58 / 1.27 | 0.91 / 1.20 | No (p 0.81 / 0.39) |
| Bottom label, payoff-weighted | 0.10 / 1.10 | 0.85 / 1.18 | No |
| Payoff label, fire at P≥0.5 | 1.28 / 1.31 | 1.34 / 1.35 | No (higher only because it trades more) |
| Payoff label, fire at P≥0.6 | 0.39 / 0.72 | 0.79 / 1.01 | No |

Holding gold over the same period: 0.93.

**Conclusion:** none of the variants times entries better than random. The payoff label at H1 is mostly noise (36% of bars start a winning trade; mean +0.011R).
